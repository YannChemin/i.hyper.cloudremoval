DESCRIPTION

i.hyper.cloudremoval detects clouds, cloud shadows, cirrus and haze in a hyperspectral 3D raster map (raster_3d) of the i.hyper module family, corrects cirrus and haze, and removes clouds and their shadows, either by setting them to NULL or by filling them from another date or from the surrounding clear pixels.

The band wavelengths, widths, validity, radiometric quantity and units, acquisition time and sun geometry come from the map's i.hyper metadata (hyper.json), as written by i.hyper.import. A map without this metadata is refused. Bands marked invalid are never used, and at filled pixels they are set to NULL.

The module works in reflectance. Top-of-atmosphere radiance (EMIT L1B, PRISMA L1, EnMAP L1B, Tanager radiance, ...) is converted with the 6SV2.1 solar spectrum (Thuillier 2003) averaged over each band's width, the Earth-Sun distance and the solar zenith; the output is written back in the units of the input. Surface reflectance (EMIT L2A, PRISMA L2D, ...) is used as it is. The radiance units are read from the metadata (uW/cm^2/SR/nm, mW/(cm^2 sr um), W/m^2/sr/nm, ...); input_scale overrides them, and also rescales reflectance stored as integers (e.g. input_scale=0.0001). If the median reflectance at 860 nm is not physical, the module stops instead of misclassifying the scene.

The solar zenith and azimuth come from sun_zenith and sun_azimuth, else from the metadata, else from the acquisition time and the centre of the computational region (NOAA solar position equations). Radiance input needs the zenith and the acquisition date. Without the azimuth, cloud shadows are found from darkness alone, with a warning.

The computational region sets the extent and the resolution of the processing; the band axis is always that of the input. A large scene can thus be processed by parts. Pixels that are NULL in every valid band, or exactly 0 in every valid band (fill written by some imports outside the swath), are no data: NULL in the mask and unchanged in the output.

Detection

Every pixel gets a bitmask (mask output):

Bit Value Class Test
0 1 cloud brightness, thin cloud, opaque cirrus (below)
1 2 shadow dark pixel inside the projected cloud shadows
2 4 water NIR < 0.05 and SWIR1 < 0.03
3 8 snow/ice NDSI and brightness (ATCOR)
4 16 cirrus 1380 nm reflectance > cirrus_threshold
5 32 haze HOT > haze_threshold
6 64 filled pixel filled by fill_method

Bits 0 to 3 match the quality map of i.hyper.atcorr -m.

  • Cloud: 470 nm reflectance above cloud_blue with NDVI below cloud_ndvi, or 860 nm reflectance above cloud_nir (Braaten et al. 2015, as i.hyper.atcorr -m), and brighter at 860 nm than at 1650 nm, which rejects bright soil and sand (ATCOR). Snow is not cloud.
  • Thin cloud (skipped with -t): the potential cloud tests of Fmask (Zhu and Woodcock 2012), with the NIR > SWIR1 test in place of Fmask's thermal test. Thin cloud grows by buffer pixels only into pixels whose 470 nm reflectance is above thin_blue, so that it takes its bright fringe and not the dark vegetation around it. On top-of-atmosphere input a thin-cloud pixel must also have an O2-A band (762 nm) at least o2_margin shallower than the clear ground within about 25 pixels: light reflected by a cloud does not cross the air below it. The reference is local so that it follows the terrain.
  • Cirrus (top-of-atmosphere input only): the 1380 nm water vapour band sees only high clouds (Gao et al. 1993). Above cirrus_cloud the cirrus is opaque and counts as cloud (ATCOR tiers).
  • Cloud shadow: dark pixels (470, 660 and 860 nm below shadow_dark, or the ATCOR cloud-shadow test) within the area that the clouds shade for cloud heights in cloud_height, sweeping the cloud mask away from the sun.
  • Objects smaller than min_size pixels are dropped; clouds and shadows then grow by buffer pixels.

input_mask replaces the cloud and shadow tests by an external mask: the bits mask_bits of its values are read as cloud (1) and shadow (2), so the quality map of i.hyper.atcorr -m can be used as it is, and a 0/1 vendor mask with mask_bits=1.

Corrections

operation chooses the corrections, applied in this order. They change only the output cube; without it the module only detects.

  • cirrus (top-of-atmosphere input only): for each band, the slope 1/Ka of the lower boundary of the scatter plot of the band against the 1380 nm reflectance is fitted, and (rho_1380 - base) / Ka is subtracted (Gao et al. 1998; Gao and Li 2017). The correction is refused if the median 1/Ka over 400-1000 nm is outside 0.5-3: the cirrus pixels then do not cover enough surface types to separate cirrus from surface.
  • haze: the haze optimized transform (HOT, Zhang et al. 2002) is the distance of a pixel above the clear line fitted to the lower boundary of the blue-red scatter plot of clear land. Hazy pixels are split into HOT levels; in each level the haze offset is the low percentile of each band minus that of the clear land (dark-object subtraction, Liang et al. 2001). The offsets follow one Angstrom law fitted over 400-700 nm, are interpolated in HOT and subtracted. HOT mistakes bright soil for haze, so haze is only flagged and removed where NIR > SWIR1. A flat offset (Angstrom exponent near 0) is thin cloud rather than aerosol; it is removed as ATCOR does, with a warning.
  • fill: removes cloud and shadow pixels with the methods of fill_method, tried in order; pixels left over become NULL.
  • null: set them to NULL.
  • reference: take them from the reference cube, an image of another date on the same bands. Its own clouds and shadows are detected and not used. Each band is matched to the input by a robust reduced-major-axis fit (ratio of interquartile ranges, matched medians) over the pixels clear in both; least squares would flatten the filled pixels whenever the dates differ in detail. A median band correlation below 0.5 gives a warning.
  • spatial: interpolate from the surrounding clear pixels. The scores of the first components principal components are interpolated by normalised convolution with a Gaussian that widens until every gap is reached, and the spectra are rebuilt from them. The fill invents data: it is refused when the gaps exceed max_fill percent of the scene.

The hot output is the HOT map, in reflectance. The output cube keeps the input's metadata, with the run in its history and the class percentages under cloud_removal in its extended metadata.

NOTES

The cube within the computational region is held in memory as 32-bit floats, next to the read and write buffers and a working copy of the bands being filled. A run on 1000 x 1000 pixels of 255 bands took 90 s and peaked at 4.3 GB: count about 4 GB per million pixels at 250 bands, and process larger scenes by parts of the region.

The detection thresholds are defined on reflectance. They hold for top-of-atmosphere and for surface reflectance; the cirrus test, the cirrus correction and the O2-A test need top-of-atmosphere data, since an atmospheric correction removes the signal they use.

Ice clouds absorb at 1.6 µm like snow, so in surface reflectance input the tops of thick ice clouds can be classed as snow/ice, which is not removed. In top-of-atmosphere input their 1380 nm signal makes them cloud.

The thin-cloud growth bound thin_blue=0.08 was chosen on EMIT L2A scenes over Belgium, checked against clear dates one week apart: 79-90% of the thin-cloud pixels were found, with 0.1-1% false positives on clear land. The o2_margin default comes from the depth of the O2-A band at the surface (about 0.3) and the 5-10% shallower band of a cloud 1 km above the ground; it removed bright-soil false positives in a radiance scene near Tehran, but has not been checked on a cloudy radiance scene.

EXAMPLES

Mask clouds and shadows, and set them to NULL:

i.hyper.cloudremoval input=emit_20230621 output=emit_20230621_cr mask=emit_20230621_mask

Only the mask and the HOT map, on a part of a large scene:

g.region n=50.86 s=50.82 w=3.99 e=4.08 -a
i.hyper.cloudremoval input=emit_20230621 mask=cloud_mask hot=hot

Fill from a clear date one week earlier, and the rest by interpolation:

i.hyper.cloudremoval input=emit_20230621 output=emit_20230621_filled \
    fill_method=reference,spatial reference=emit_20230614

Top-of-atmosphere radiance: correct cirrus and haze, then remove clouds:

i.hyper.cloudremoval input=emit_l1b output=emit_l1b_cr mask=emit_l1b_mask \
    operation=cirrus,haze,fill

Use the quality map of i.hyper.atcorr instead of the cloud tests:

i.hyper.atcorr input=emit_l1b ... -m quality=atcorr_quality
i.hyper.cloudremoval input=emit_l1b output=emit_l1b_cr input_mask=atcorr_quality mask_bits=3
